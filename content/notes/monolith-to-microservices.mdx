Big-bang rewrites are where roadmaps go to die. The honest version of "we migrated to
microservices" is eighteen months of a [[feature-flags-that-scale]] quietly routing one
capability at a time away from the monolith until there's nothing left to route.

## Route, don't rewrite

The strangler-fig pattern borrows its name from a vine that grows around a host tree and
eventually replaces it. In code, the "vine" is a routing layer: new requests check a
per-capability flag; if it's on they hit the v2 service, otherwise they fall through to legacy.

```kotlin
// orders.v2 grows; the monolith shrinks
fun place(cmd: PlaceOrder) =
  if (flags.on("orders.v2", cmd.tenant)) orders.place(cmd)
  else legacy.place(cmd)
```

Every seam you cut buys independence and costs a network hop plus a consistency problem — see
the [[database-per-service-tax]] before you cut the third one.

## The three seams worth cutting first

- The write path with the worst lock contention — moving it out buys the most headroom.
- The capability with a different scaling shape (read-heavy catalog beside a write-heavy ledger).
- The thing a separate team owns — Conway's law is cheaper to obey than to fight.

A monolith with three clean seams beats a distributed system with twelve blurry ones, and
[[idempotency-keys]] is what keeps the hops honest while you get there.
