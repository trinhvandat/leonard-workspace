Most "agent" systems are workflows wearing a trench coat, and that's a compliment. The
reliable ones are mostly plumbing: prompt-chaining, routing, and the occasional loop —
each earning its complexity or getting cut.

## Start with a chain

Prompt-chaining — one step's output is the next step's input — handles the majority of
real tasks and is trivial to debug because every step is inspectable. Reach for routing
only when inputs genuinely fork into different handlers.

## When the loop earns its keep

The one pattern worth the extra moving parts is the [[evaluator-optimizer-loop]]: a
generator proposes, an evaluator grades, and you iterate until the score stops improving.
It's worth it when quality is measurable and a single pass is reliably not good enough —
and a waste everywhere else.
