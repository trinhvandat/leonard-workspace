A feature flag starts life as an `if`. It ends life as a second, undocumented
configuration system that nobody is allowed to delete. The gap between those two is
where the engineering actually lives.

## Targeting, not toggling

A flag that's only on or off can't do a staged rollout. The useful unit is a targeting
rule: on for this tenant, this percentage, this region. That's what lets a
[[monolith-to-microservices]] migration move one customer at a time instead of all of them
on a Friday afternoon.

## Keep the kill switch honest

- Every flag needs an owner and an expiry — a flag with neither is debt with a UI.
- A kill switch must fail safe: if the flag service is unreachable, fall back to the
  last-known or the conservative value, never crash.
- Audit flag reads the same way you audit config, because by month six that's what they are.

The discipline isn't the flags; it's deleting them. A flag that outlived its rollout is a
branch in production that no test covers.
