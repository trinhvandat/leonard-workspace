Owning your data per service is the right call — it's also the one that quietly sends
an invoice every month after you make it. The pattern is sound; the budget line is missing.

## What you're actually paying for

The moment a service owns its own schema, the join you used to write in SQL becomes an
API call, a cache, and a stale-data bug waiting to happen. Reports that spanned three
tables now span three services and a nightly export.

- **Joins** become composition in application code, or a read model you maintain by hand.
- **Reports** need a separate analytics copy, because nobody wants the ledger service
  answering ad-hoc BI queries.
- **Transactions** across services don't exist — you reach for event-driven sagas and
  compensating actions instead of `BEGIN; ... COMMIT;`.

## Pay it on purpose

None of this is a reason to keep the monolith's single shared database. It's a reason to
cut services where the data boundary is real, not where the org chart is loud — the same
discipline that makes [[monolith-to-microservices]] work. Budget the tax up front and it's
an investment; discover it in production and it's a tax audit.
